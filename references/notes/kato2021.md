## What the source says

- "The degree of shrinkage was proportional to the cell size before cell death, and it was 27.2% and 27.0% in the area in W303 and BY4741 strains, respectively" (Results, paragraph on Fig. 2).
- "Less than 1% of surviving cells shrank more than 10% in the area during the time-lapse period" (same paragraph).
- "the cell area shrinks 25 to 28% on average" (Discussion).

## Sample and design

Time-lapse microscopy of single cells of two laboratory strains (W303, BY4741) taken from stationary-phase batch culture in defined medium, on agarose pads with conditioned medium, 30 h at 1-h intervals; death marked by propidium iodide (and phloxine B as a second stain). 669 cells at the start in the W303 run; shrinkage curves are means of 93 cells from three independent experiments (Fig. 2A).

## Verdict: matches

The number and the direction are right: a cell loses about 27 % of its area when it dies. One wording point: the 27 % is shrinkage at death, not a property of stationary-phase cells in general; surviving stationary-phase cells did not shrink. "dying/stationary-phase cells" should read "cells dying in stationary phase". The source is laboratory strains under a research microscope, not baker's yeast through a phone.

## Use for the crowdfunding page

Usable as a plain fact: "a yeast cell loses about a quarter of its area when it dies" (Kato et al. 2021, mBio 12:e03094-21, Results). It supports the campaign's caution that smaller cells may simply be dying cells.
